Homes and interiors

Shailesh designs a house as a whole. Each room is planned for the people who will use it and drawn before work starts. It is finished with wall effects, furniture and paintings made for that room. Even a small powder room gets its own character.

He has designed around ten houses in Ahmedabad and Mumbai, about five in Southern California and about five in Northern California, and renovated four hotels between 2002 and 2012. Four of the houses are shown in full below.

Talk about a project

Shailesh Joshi

Shailesh (Sylesh) Joshi is a painter, muralist, printmaker, teacher and designer. He trained and taught in Ahmedabad, exhibited across India, designed about twenty houses and renovated four hotels in two countries, and has returned to concentrated studio work in the United States.

Artist statement

My work begins with emotion.

I have spent much of my life observing people—their expressions, gestures, movements, and quiet moments. I am interested not simply in reproducing what I see, but in finding the emotion that exists beneath the visible surface.

Working primarily in oil and watercolor, I use color, form, and expression to create an experience that is felt before it is explained. My years as an artist, educator, and designer have taught me to observe carefully, but I do not want observation to dictate a single interpretation.

I believe the relationship between artist and viewer is essential to the life of a painting. I may begin a work with my own emotions, but I do not believe its story belongs entirely to me. Each viewer arrives with different memories, experiences, and sensitivities. What one person discovers in a painting may be entirely different from what another sees.

That is where art becomes alive.

I admire artists such as Mark Rothko for demonstrating the extraordinary capacity of art to communicate emotion without requiring explanation. I similarly seek an emotional connection that extends beyond literal representation or predetermined narrative.

I do not want to tell the viewer what to feel.

I want the work to awaken something within them.

The art itself should become the story.

Painting from memory

Shailesh paints from memory. He watches his surroundings closely, the details, the expressions, the light and the textures, and brings them back to life later in the studio. Art, for him, is a way to express memories as he sees them, or as he would like to see them.

He works in oil, watercolor and acrylic, and moves into graphic design and interior design when a project calls for it.

Life and work

Ahmedabad

Learning and teaching

Shailesh trained at Chimanlal Nagindas Fine Arts College in Ahmedabad. He took a first-class diploma in painting and then a master's degree in fine arts, finishing first in the state. His studies included three fresco techniques and mural painting, and he learned printmaking in several methods.

The college offered him a teaching post as soon as he graduated, and he went on to lead its painting department as Head of Department. He taught still life, portraiture from life and landscape on site, in oil, watercolor and charcoal, along with abstract and figurative painting, composition, printmaking, mosaic and Indian fresco technique.

He wrote the curriculum for several courses and took students out on field studies of the human figure and of nature. Students often joined the field trips he made for his own work, and he is still in touch with some of them.

India

Exhibitions and awards

Alongside teaching he kept up a studio practice and showed regularly. He held solo exhibitions at Jehangir Art Gallery in Mumbai, at Contemporary Art Gallery in Ahmedabad and twice at the College of Fine Arts in Ahmedabad, and took part in group exhibitions in Ahmedabad, Mumbai and New Delhi.

He won more than a dozen awards, among them the Gujarat State Fine Art Academy Award for Drawing, the Bombay Art Society National Award, including one for printmaking, and the Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation Art Exhibition Award.

His paintings and prints are held by the National Museum of Modern Art in New Delhi and the State Art Academy in Ahmedabad, and he made work for the Palace on Wheels.

On paper

Printmaking and drawing

Printmaking runs through the early work. The plates show small figures standing in an open field, a body lying among leaves, an empty chair in tall grass, a couple holding each other under a dark sky. That last print is titled Desolation.

The drawings are quick and spare: ink figures, wash studies of old houses, and small abstract works in black, green and rust. He studied printmaking in several methods and later taught it, and he was selected for a book on the hundred best printmakers of the past hundred years.

A lifelong subject

Mahatma Gandhi

Gandhi is the subject Shailesh returns to most. He has said he is deeply impressed by Gandhi's ideas of truth and nonviolence, and that the portraits are his way of carrying that message to others.

He has painted Gandhi many times: laughing, walking with his staff, seated, head bowed. Fourteen of the portraits are shown here.

Design and building

Homes and interiors

In 2001 Shailesh began working as an interior designer and artist with Art Effects in San Francisco and Los Angeles. Houses were designed from scratch, with the raw material bought and the furniture made for each one. They included homes in Cupertino, Redwood City and Atherton, a 9,000 square foot house on a hilltop in San Francisco, and a 12,000 square foot house in Los Angeles.

In Los Angeles he planned most of the rooms and chose the color scheme, flooring, bathrooms and wall effects, then selected and placed the furniture and paintings, working alongside the owner, the architect and the construction crew.

In Ahmedabad he designed houses of 7,000 and 9,000 square feet from the start of construction, with everything made on site. At Alekh Designs he led a workshop of fifteen people and worked with stone carvers, sculptors, painters and finishers. He has also designed restaurants, shops and offices.

In all he has designed around ten houses in Ahmedabad and Mumbai, about five in Southern California and about five in Northern California, and has completed more than 200 interior and design projects. Between 2002 and 2012 he renovated four hotels. For one in Los Angeles he designed the furniture to the franchise standard, had it made in India and imported it.

On the wall

Murals and art effects

Painting and building meet in his wall work. Shailesh paints figure murals, ceilings, faux finishes and cloud effects, often for the same houses he designs: a lake scene across a living room wall in Los Angeles, a world map in metal leaf on a library ceiling, a ring of dancers above a home temple, an open sky over an entry hall in San Francisco.

He has also painted a mural in Chicago and made a mosaic mural for Crown TV. He visualizes each effect before starting and does not repeat it. Over his career he has made many hundreds of paintings, from small watercolors to murals that fill a room.

In the workshop

Furniture made for the room

The furniture in his houses is designed for a particular room and built by hand. There is a carved wooden swing for a family room, a dining table that rests on carved peacocks, round glass tables on stone bases shaped like lotus flowers, a black cabinet with gilded lion heads, and mirrors in hand-carved gilt frames.

In Ahmedabad the pieces were made on site by his own team. For clients abroad, furniture was made in India and shipped.

In print

Graphic design

Between 2006 and 2008 Shailesh designed the printed material for The Da Vinci Experience, an exhibit of Leonardo da Vinci's machines shown at the Palm Springs Air Museum from November 2006 to February 2007.

The work covered book covers, the exhibit catalog, a teacher's guide, a timeline, packaging for a sticker sheet and a carrier for the exhibit's Chianti wine. A later cover, from 2008, was designed for the book Courting Failure.

United States

A return to the studio

In recent years Shailesh has returned to concentrated studio work. His paintings were part of Darshan: Visions of India at the Tennessee Valley Museum of Art in Alabama, and of the traveling exhibition that followed in 2023 and 2024.

In 2025 he showed in a two-person exhibition at Gallery at El Sendero in Santa Fe, New Mexico, and joined an artists' retreat in Alabama. He is a member of the Indian American Artists Association.

How he works

He listens first

Many clients are unsure what they want. He sits with them, learns what each member of the family needs, and helps them picture the house before any work is done.

He draws it

Rooms are sketched for the client, and most effects are visualized before work starts. He describes himself as detail oriented and well planned.

He does not repeat

Every area is planned separately, around its use and the people in it. Each effect is one of a kind, and even a small powder room is treated as a whole.
